### Lesson 1: Word order: who does what
- Chinese sentences follow the same basic order as English: subject, verb, object. 我喝茶 means 'I drink tea'.
- Verbs never change. There is no past-tense ending, no plural and no 'he drinks' form. 我喝, 他喝 and 我们喝 all use the same 喝.
- Words that say when and where come before the verb, not after it. 我昨天在家 means 'I was at home yesterday'. 爸爸晚上在家工作 means 'Dad works at home in the evening'. Time usually comes first, then place.
- (common mistake) Do not put the place or the time after the verb the way English does. Say 我在家吃饭 (I eat at home), not 我吃饭在家.
- example: 我喝茶。 / I drink tea.
- example: 哥哥吃鸡蛋。 / My older brother eats eggs.
- example: 我们学习汉语。 / We study Chinese.
- example: 爸爸晚上在家工作。 / Dad works at home in the evening.

### Lesson 2: 是: A is B
- 是 links two nouns: 他是我的朋友 means 'He is my friend'. 我是学生 means 'I am a student'. It works like 'is / am / are' with nouns.
- Do not use 是 before an adjective. Say 她很漂亮, not 她是很漂亮 (see the lesson on describing things).
- To say no, put 不 in front: 我不是老师。 To ask a yes/no question, add 吗: 你是学生吗？
- (common mistake) Do not put 是 before an adjective: 今天很冷 is right, 今天是很冷 is wrong.
- example: 我是学生。 / I am a student.
- example: 他是我的朋友。 / He is my friend.
- example: 她不是老师。 / She is not a teacher.
- example: 你是中国人吗？ / Are you Chinese?

### Lesson 3: Describing things: 很, 太, 真, 非常, 有点儿
- An adjective can be the whole predicate. There is no 是: 今天很冷 means 'It is cold today'.
- Usually 很 goes before the adjective. In a plain statement it often means just 'is' rather than a strong 'very': 她很忙. A bare adjective with nothing in front, like 她忙, sounds like a comparison ('she is busy, but others are not').
- For stronger feeling use 非常 (very, extremely), 真 (really) or 太…了 (too, so): 今天太冷了！ 有点儿 means 'a bit' and is often used for something you do not like: 今天有点儿冷.
- To say not, put 不 before the adjective and leave out 很: 这个房间不大。
- (common mistake) Do not use 是 with an adjective: 她很漂亮, not 她是很漂亮.
- (common mistake) 太…了 can mean 'too' (太贵了, too expensive) or 'so / how' (太好了！ That is great!).
- example: 今天很冷。 / It is very cold today.
- example: 这个房间不大。 / This room is not big.
- example: 你的衣服真漂亮！ / Your clothes are really pretty!
- example: 今天太冷了！ / It is so cold today!
- example: 我今天有点儿忙。 / I am a bit busy today.

### Lesson 4: Yes/no questions with 吗
- Say a normal statement and add 吗 at the end: 你是学生吗？ The word order does not change.
- Answer with the verb: 你喝茶吗？ — 喝。 or 不喝。 For 是 questions, answer 是 or 不是.
- A plain verb + 吗 question asks about a fact or a habit. 你喝茶吗？ means 'Do you drink tea?' It is not an offer. To offer something or ask what someone would like, use 想: 你想喝茶吗？ (see the lesson on 想 and 要).
- Do not use 吗 together with a question word: say 你叫什么名字？ (not 你叫什么名字吗？).
- (common mistake) If 什么, 谁 or 哪儿 is already asking the question, leave 吗 off.
- (common mistake) 你喝茶吗？ asks whether you drink tea in general. To offer tea, say 你想喝茶吗？
- example: 你是老师吗？ / Are you a teacher?
- example: 他喜欢茶吗？ / Does he like tea?
- example: 你想喝水吗？ / Would you like some water?
- example: 你认识他吗？ / Do you know him?

### Lesson 5: Question words: 什么, 谁, 哪儿, 几, 多少, 怎么
- The question word goes where the answer would go. 你吃什么？ is answered with 我吃米饭. 她是谁？ is answered with 她是我的老师.
- Common ones: 什么 (what), 谁 (who), 哪儿 or 哪里 (where), 哪 + measure word (which: 哪个, 哪本书), 几 (how many, small numbers, with a measure word), 多少 (how many, how much), 怎么 (how: 这个字怎么读), 怎么样 (how about it, how is it), 什么时候 (when).
- Do not add 吗 to these questions.
- (common mistake) Use 几 for small numbers and put a measure word after it (几口人, 几岁). Use 多少 for bigger or unknown amounts, like 多少钱.
- example: 你想吃什么？ / What do you want to eat?
- example: 她是谁？ / Who is she?
- example: 你家在哪儿？ / Where is your home?
- example: 你家有几口人？ / How many people are in your family?
- example: 这个多少钱？ / How much is this?
- example: 这个字怎么读？ / How do you read this character?
- example: 今天天气怎么样？ / How is the weather today?

### Lesson 6: Saying no: 不, 没(有), 不要
- 不 negates most things: 是, adjectives, habits, plans and wanting. 我不是老师。我不喝茶。这个不大。
- 没(有) negates 有 and things that did not happen: 我没有钱。小明今天没上学。Never say 不有. 'Have' is always negated with 没.
- 不要 is a command, 'don't!': 不要说话！ It can also be a firm refusal. For a plain 'I don't want to', 不想 is the safe choice: 我不想吃。
- Tone change: 不 is said bú before a fourth-tone syllable. 不是 is bú shì, and 不要 is búyào.
- (common mistake) Do not say 我不有钱. 'Have' is negated with 没: 我没有钱.
- (common mistake) 不要 is 'don't!'. For 'I don't want to', say 我不想…
- example: 我不是老师。 / I am not a teacher.
- example: 我没有钱。 / I don't have any money.
- example: 小明今天没上学。 / Xiaoming did not go to school today.
- example: 不要在这儿说话。 / Don't talk here.

### Lesson 7: 有: having something
- 有 means 'to have': 我有一个哥哥。我有两本书。
- The negative is 没有, never 不有: 我没有手机。
- Ask with 吗, or with 有没有: 你有手机吗？ or 你有没有手机？
- 有 is also used for 'there is / there are'. See the lesson on places and positions.
- (common mistake) Do not add 吗 to 有没有: say 你有没有手机？ or 你有手机吗？, not both.
- example: 我有一个哥哥。 / I have an older brother.
- example: 我没有手机。 / I don't have a phone.
- example: 你有时间吗？ / Do you have time?
- example: 她有没有男朋友？ / Does she have a boyfriend?

### Lesson 8: Pronouns and 们
- 我 I, 你 you, 他 he, 她 she, 它 it. Plurals: 我们 we, 你们 you (more than one), 他们 they (people, or a mixed group), 她们 they (all women), 它们 they (things or animals). He, she and it all sound like tā.
- 您 is a polite 'you' for one person: 老师，您好！ 大家 means 'everyone'.
- 们 makes plurals for people: 同学们, 孩子们. Do not use 们 after a number: 三个学生, not 三个学生们.
- 小 in front of a name makes it friendly, like 小明.
- (common mistake) Do not add 们 after a number: 三个学生 is right.
- example: 我们都是学生。 / We are all students.
- example: 他们在学校。 / They are at school.
- example: 老师，您好！ / Hello, teacher!
- example: 同学们好！ / Hello, classmates!
- example: 大家好！ / Hello, everyone!

### Lesson 9: 的: belonging and describing
- 的 links an owner or a describing phrase to a noun: 我妈妈的手机 means 'my mom's phone'. 好吃的面条儿 means 'tasty noodles'.
- With family and close groups, 的 is usually left out: 我妈妈, 我家, 我们学校. For things, use it: 我的书, 我的手机.
- The noun can be dropped when it is clear: 这是我的。 means 'This is mine.'
- A short adjective often joins the noun directly: 新手机 (a new phone). With 很 or a longer phrase, add 的: 很好吃的面条儿 (very tasty noodles).
- (common mistake) 我的妈妈 is not wrong, but in everyday speech people say 我妈妈.
- example: 这是我的书。 / This is my book.
- example: 这是我妈妈的手机。 / This is my mother's phone.
- example: 我妈妈在家。 / My mom is at home.
- example: 这是很好吃的面条儿。 / These are very tasty noodles.

### Lesson 10: 这, 那 and measure words
- 这 means this and 那 means that. Before a noun they need a measure word: 这个人 (this person), 那本书 (that book). 这个 and 那个 can also stand alone: 我喜欢这个。 Plurals are 这些 and 那些. Places are 这儿 / 这里 (here) and 那儿 / 那里 (there).
- Chinese counts with a measure word between the number and the noun: 三个苹果, 两本书. The all-purpose one is 个.
- Some nouns want a specific one: 本 for books, 件 for clothes (and for matters), 只 (zhī) for small animals, 口 for people in a family, 家 for shops and companies, 块 or 元 for money, and 杯 for cups of drinks.
- Ask how many with 几 + measure word: 你有几本书？
- (common mistake) Use the right measure word: 三本书, not 三个书. Use 个 only when no special one fits.
- example: 这个人是我的朋友。 / This person is my friend.
- example: 我有两本书。 / I have two books.
- example: 我想买一件新衣服。 / I want to buy a new piece of clothing.
- example: 我家有四口人。 / There are four people in my family.
- example: 我想喝一杯茶。 / I would like a cup of tea.

### Lesson 11: Numbers, 两, money, age and 第
- 一 二 三 四 五 六 七 八 九 十. Then 十一 (11), 二十 (20), 二十一 (21). 一百 is 100 and 一百零五 is 105 (零 fills the gap). 一千 is 1,000.
- There are two words for two: 二 for counting, numbers and dates (二十, 二月), and 两 before a measure word (两个, 两本书).
- 半 means half: 一半 (half of it). Money uses 块 (spoken) or 元 (written), often with 钱: 三块钱, 三十元. One and a half yuan is 一块半.
- 第 makes an ordinal number: 第一天 means 'the first day'.
- Age uses 岁 and needs no 是: 我八岁。
- (common mistake) Say 两个人, not 二个人. But 二 is right in numbers and dates: 二十, 二月二号.
- example: 这个手机三千块。 / This phone is 3,000 yuan.
- example: 我有两个朋友。 / I have two friends.
- example: 他八岁。 / He is eight years old.
- example: 这个包子一块半。 / This bun is one and a half yuan.
- example: 今天是我学汉语的第一天。 / Today is my first day of learning Chinese.

### Lesson 12: Time, dates and days
- Clock time is number + 点 (o'clock), then + 分 (minutes) or 半 (half): 七点, 八点零五分, 七点半. Say 两点 for 2 o'clock.
- Dates go from big to small: year, month, day. Say 月 and 号 (spoken) or 日 (written): 十月三十号. Weekdays are 星期 + number: 星期一 to 星期六. Sunday is 星期天 or 星期日. Ask with 几: 今天星期几？ 现在几点？
- Time words go before the verb, either right after the subject or at the start of the sentence: 我七点起床 or 七点我起床. Not after the verb.
- A time or date does not need a verb: 现在七点半。 With 是 it also works: 现在是八点零五分。
- (common mistake) Say 两点 for 2 o'clock, not 二点.
- (common mistake) Time goes before the verb: 我七点起床, not 我起床七点.
- example: 我七点起床。 / I get up at seven o'clock.
- example: 现在是八点零五分。 / It is 8:05 now.
- example: 今天是十月三十号。 / Today is October 30th.
- example: 今天星期几？ / What day of the week is it today?
- example: 我星期天在家。 / I am at home on Sunday.

### Lesson 13: 在: where something happens
- 在 means 'at / in'. 在 + place goes before the verb: 我在家吃饭 means 'I eat at home'.
- 在 can also be a verb, 'to be at': 我在家。他不在学校。
- Ask where with 在哪儿 or 在哪里: 你在哪儿工作？
- (common mistake) The place goes before the verb: 我在家吃饭, not 我吃饭在家.
- example: 我在家。 / I am at home.
- example: 他们在家吃饭。 / They eat at home.
- example: 你在哪儿工作？ / Where do you work?
- example: 我在学校学习汉语。 / I study Chinese at school.

### Lesson 14: Positions and where things are
- 上 (on) and 里 (inside) go right after the noun: 桌子上, 房间里. For under, outside, in front and behind, add 边 (side): 桌子下边, 房间外边, 学校前边, 学校后边. 这边 and 那边 work the same way.
- Thing + 在 + place says where the thing is: 书在桌子上。
- Place + 有 + thing says what is there: 桌子上有一本书。
- Place + 是 + thing identifies what is there: 我家外边是一家商店 (What is outside my home is a shop). 有 only says that something exists there.
- (common mistake) Do not use 在 for 'there is': 桌子上有一本书, not 桌子上在一本书.
- example: 书在桌子上。 / The book is on the table.
- example: 杯子里有水。 / There is water in the cup.
- example: 桌子上有一本书。 / There is a book on the table.
- example: 我家外边是一家商店。 / Outside my home there is a shop.
- example: 学校在那边。 / The school is over there.

### Lesson 15: 想 and 要: wanting and offering
- 想 + verb means 'want to' or 'would like to': 我想喝茶。 It is soft and polite, so it is the word to use when you offer or ask what someone wants: 你想喝茶吗？ (Would you like some tea?) 你想吃什么？ (What would you like to eat?)
- 要 also means 'want', but it is more direct. It works with a noun, which is how you order or buy: 我要一个包子。 With a verb: 我要休息。
- A plain verb question is not an offer. 你喝茶吗？ means 'Do you drink tea?' and asks about a habit. To ask 'Would you like tea?', add 想: 你想喝茶吗？
- The negative of 想 is 不想: 我不想吃。 不要 is 'don't!' or a firm refusal.
- (common mistake) 你喝茶吗？ is not an offer. It asks whether you drink tea. Offer with 你想喝茶吗？
- (common mistake) 不想 is 'do not want to'. 不要 is 'don't!'
- example: 我想去中国。 / I want to go to China.
- example: 你想喝什么？ / What would you like to drink?
- example: 你想喝茶吗？ / Would you like some tea?
- example: 我要一个包子。 / I'll have a bun.
- example: 我不想吃东西。 / I don't want anything to eat.

### Lesson 16: 会, 能, 可以: can
- 会: you know how to, a skill you learned. 我会做饭。你会开车吗？
- 能: you are able to, or the situation allows it. 我明天能来。你能来吗？
- 可以: you are allowed to. It is how you ask for permission: 我可以坐这儿吗？ To say yes, answer 可以.
- They overlap: 能 is also used to ask permission. The negatives are 不会 (don't know how), 不能 (can't) and 不可以 (not allowed).
- (common mistake) Do not use 会 to ask permission: 我可以坐这儿吗？, not 我会坐这儿吗？
- example: 我会说一点儿汉语。 / I can speak a little Chinese.
- example: 你会开车吗？ / Can you drive?
- example: 你明天能来吗？ / Can you come tomorrow?
- example: 我可以坐这儿吗？ / May I sit here?

### Lesson 17: 都, 也, 还, 再 and joining sentences
- These adverbs go after the subject and before the verb or adjective: 我们都是学生。我也是学生。
- 都 means all or both: 我们都很好。 With 不, it means none of us: 我们都不喝茶。
- 也 means also or too. It goes before 不: 我也不是学生 means 'I am not a student either'. 我也是 means 'Me too'. Do not put 也 at the end of a sentence.
- 还 means also, else or still: 你还想吃什么？ (What else would you like to eat?)
- 再 means again, for something that has not happened yet: 明天再来。
- Two short sentences can sit side by side with a comma and no 'and': 爸爸是老师，妈妈也是老师。
- (common mistake) 也 goes before the verb: 我也喜欢茶, not 我喜欢茶也.
- (common mistake) 都 goes after the subject: 他们都在学校, not 都他们在学校.
- example: 我们都是学生。 / We are all students.
- example: 我也是学生。 / I am a student too.
- example: 你还想吃什么？ / What else would you like to eat?
- example: 明天再来。 / Come again tomorrow.
- example: 爸爸是老师，妈妈也是老师。 / Dad is a teacher, and Mom is a teacher too.

### Lesson 18: A-not-A questions
- Another way to ask a yes/no question: say the verb, then 不, then the verb again. 你是不是老师？ 你去不去学校？ (Are you going to school or not?)
- For 有, use 没: 你有没有手机？
- Do not add 吗 to an A-not-A question.
- (common mistake) No 吗 here: 你去不去？, not 你去不去吗？
- example: 她是不是你们的老师？ / Is she your teacher?
- example: 你去不去学校？ / Are you going to school?
- example: 你有没有手机？ / Do you have a phone?
- example: 你喜欢不喜欢茶？ / Do you like tea?

### Lesson 19: 吧 and 呢
- 吧 softens a sentence. After a suggestion it means 'let's' or 'how about': 我们去吃饭吧。 It also makes a request gentle: 你喝水吧 (Have some water).
- 吧 can also check what you already think is true, like 'right?': 你没事吧？ (You are all right, aren't you?)
- 呢 asks 'and what about…?' by bouncing a question back: 我很好，你呢？ (I am fine, and you?)
- 呢 can also ask where something is: 你的手机呢？ (Where is your phone?)
- (common mistake) Use 吗 when you really want a yes/no answer. 吧 suggests or checks, 吗 asks.
- example: 我们去吃饭吧。 / Let's go and eat.
- example: 你喝水吧。 / Have some water.
- example: 我很好，你呢？ / I am fine, and you?
- example: 你的手机呢？ / Where is your phone?
- example: 你没事吧？ / Are you all right?

### Lesson 20: 在, 正在 and 呢: happening now
- Put 在 or 正在 before a verb to say it is happening right now: 爸爸在做饭。他正在看电视。
- 呢 at the end is optional and sounds lively: 他正在看书呢。 You can even use 呢 alone: 小明睡觉呢。
- Be careful: 在 + place means 'at' (我在家), and 在 + verb means 'in the middle of' (我在看书).
- For habits, use the plain verb: 我早上喝茶 (I drink tea in the morning).
- (common mistake) For a habit, do not use 在: 我早上喝茶, not 我在早上喝茶.
- example: 爸爸在做饭，妈妈在看书。 / Dad is cooking and Mom is reading.
- example: 他正在看电视。 / He is watching TV right now.
- example: 哥哥正在看书呢。 / My older brother is reading right now.
- example: 小明睡觉呢。 / Xiaoming is sleeping.

### Lesson 21: 了 after a verb: it's done
- 了 right after a verb says the action is finished: 我买了一本书。 (I bought a book.)
- It is not a past-tense marker. It marks 'completed'. Do not use it for habits (我早上喝茶, not 我早上喝了茶) or for plain facts about a state (我昨天在家).
- A short sentence like 我买了书 can sound unfinished. Add an amount or a describing word: 我买了一本书, 妈妈喝了一杯茶. Or put 了 at the end: 我买书了。
- To say it did not happen, use 没(有) and drop 了: 我没买书。
- Ask with 吗: 你吃饭了吗？
- (common mistake) Do not add 了 to habits: 我早上喝茶 (not 我早上喝了茶).
- (common mistake) Do not keep 了 in the negative: 我没买书 (not 我没买了书).
- example: 我买了一本书。 / I bought a book.
- example: 妈妈喝了一杯茶。 / Mom drank a cup of tea.
- example: 我看见他了。 / I saw him.
- example: 我没买书。 / I did not buy a book.
- example: 你吃饭了吗？ / Have you eaten?

### Lesson 22: 了 at the end: something has changed
- 了 at the end of a sentence says 'now it is like this': a change or a new situation. 天冷了 means 'It has turned cold'. 下雨了 means 'It is raining now'. 我八岁了 means 'I am eight now'.
- A sentence like this does not need a subject: 下雨了！ 下课了！
- Compare 今天很冷 (a plain fact, it is cold today) with 天冷了 (news: it has turned cold).
- 太…了 is a separate pattern: 太好了！ (That is great!)
- example: 天冷了。 / It has turned cold.
- example: 下雨了！ / It is raining now!
- example: 现在八点了。 / It is eight o'clock now.
- example: 他病了。 / He has fallen ill.
- example: 下课了，我们去吃饭。 / Class is over, let's go and eat.

### Lesson 23: 和 and 对
- 和 joins nouns and pronouns, like 'and': 我和爸爸, 老师和学生。 To link two whole sentences, use a comma instead.
- With 都, put 都 after the whole group: 我和他都是学生。
- 对 + person + verb means 'to / toward someone': 他对我很好。我对他说：'你好！'
- 对 also means 'right, correct': 对，我是学生。
- (common mistake) 都 comes after the group: 我和他都是学生, not 我都和他是学生.
- example: 我和他是同学。 / He and I are classmates.
- example: 我和爸爸都喜欢看书。 / Dad and I both like reading.
- example: 他对我很好。 / He is very nice to me.
- example: 对，我是学生。 / Yes, that's right, I am a student.

### Lesson 24: Two actions in a row, and giving
- Chinese lines up verbs in the order they happen: 我去商店买水果 means 'I go to the shop to buy fruit'. The second action is the purpose of the first.
- Sometimes the first action is how you do the second: 爸爸开车去公司 (Dad drives to work), 我坐火车回家 (I take the train home).
- Giving and asking can take two objects, the person first: 爸爸给小明一本书 (Dad gives Xiaoming a book). 我问老师一个问题 (I ask the teacher a question).
- 给 + person before a verb means 'for / to someone': 我给妈妈打电话 (I call my mom).
- (common mistake) Keep the order of events: 我去商店买水果, not 我买水果去商店.
- example: 我去商店买水果。 / I go to the shop to buy fruit.
- example: 爸爸开车去公司。 / Dad drives to work.
- example: 爸爸给小明一本书。 / Dad gives Xiaoming a book.
- example: 我给妈妈打电话。 / I call my mom.

### Lesson 25: 请, 一下 and 喂
- 请 + verb means 'please': 请坐。请喝茶。 请问 means 'may I ask…?' and opens a question: 请问，学校在哪儿？
- Verb + 一下 means 'for a moment' or 'a little': 我问一下老师。我想休息一下。 It also makes a request softer.
- 喂 is how you answer or start a phone call: 喂，你好！ 喂，请问小明在家吗？
- Some verbs already include an object (睡觉, 上课, 说话). Put 了 after the whole word: 他们上课了。 When another action follows, 了 may move inside: 下了课，我们去吃饭。
- (common mistake) 请 comes before the verb: 请坐, not 坐请.
- example: 请喝茶。 / Please have some tea.
- example: 请问，学校在哪儿？ / Excuse me, where is the school?
- example: 我想休息一下。 / I want to rest for a bit.
- example: 喂，你好！ / Hello! (answering the phone)

### Lesson 26: Some and a little: 一些, 一点儿, 有些, 有的, 有点儿
- 一些 means 'some' or 'a few' and goes before a noun: 我想买一些水果。
- 一点儿 means 'a little' or 'a bit' of something: 我会说一点儿汉语。
- 有些 and 有的 mean 'some (of them)' and start a sentence about part of a group: 有些学生不喜欢读书。有的人喜欢喝茶，有的人喜欢喝牛奶。
- 有点儿 is different. It goes before an adjective and means 'a bit', often about something you do not like: 今天有点儿冷。
- (common mistake) 有点儿 goes before an adjective: 今天有点儿冷. Say 我会说一点儿汉语 for 'a little Chinese'.
- example: 我想买一些水果。 / I want to buy some fruit.
- example: 我会说一点儿汉语。 / I can speak a little Chinese.
- example: 有些学生不喜欢读书。 / Some students don't like studying.
- example: 今天有点儿冷。 / It is a bit cold today.
