Lessons:

### Lesson 1: 把: doing something to something
- 把 puts the object in front of the verb, so you can say what you do to it and what becomes of it: 我把门关上了 'I closed the door'. The pattern is Subject + 把 + object + verb + something extra (a result, a place, or 了).
- The verb cannot stand alone after 把. Add a result, a place, a direction or 了: 请把门打开 'Please open the door'; 请把书放在桌子上 'Please put the book on the table'; 我把作业做完了 'I have finished my homework'.
- The object after 把 should be something known (the, this, that), not 'a' something. Say 把这本书给我 'Give me this book', not 把一本书给我.
- A negative goes before 把: 我没把作业做完 'I did not finish the homework'; 别把手机丢了 'Don't lose your phone'.
- (common mistake) Do not use 把 with a bare verb: 我把书看 is wrong. Add a result or 了: 我把书看完了 'I have finished the book'.
- (common mistake) The negative goes before 把, not before the verb: 我没把作业做完, not 我把作业没做完.
- example: 请把门打开。 / Please open the door.
- example: 我把作业做完了。 / I have finished my homework.
- example: 请把书放在桌子上。 / Please put the book on the table.
- example: 他把杯子打坏了。 / He has broken the cup.

### Lesson 2: 被: something is done to it
- 被 turns the sentence around. Instead of 'he broke the cup' (他打坏了杯子) you can say what happened to the cup: 杯子被他打坏了 'The cup was broken by him'.
- The pattern is Thing + 被 + (doer) + verb + result or 了. Like 把, the verb needs something extra. The doer can be left out: 我的书被借走了 'My book has been borrowed'.
- 被 is most often used when something unwanted or unexpected happens to the thing: 我的伞被朋友拿走了 'My umbrella was taken away by a friend'. In speech 让 and 叫 can replace 被: 杯子让他打坏了.
- A negative goes before 被: 我的书没被借走 'My book was not borrowed'.
- (common mistake) Do not use a bare verb after 被: 杯子被他打 is incomplete. Say 杯子被他打坏了.
- (common mistake) The negative goes before 被: 我的书没被借走, not 我的书被没借走.
- example: 杯子被他打坏了。 / The cup was broken by him.
- example: 我的伞被朋友拿走了。 / My umbrella was taken away by a friend.
- example: 作业被老师收走了。 / The homework was collected by the teacher.
- example: 我的书被同学借走了。 / My book was borrowed by a classmate.

### Lesson 3: 如果…就… and 的话: if
- 如果 means 'if'. The condition comes first and the result second, and the result usually has 就 (or 会) before its verb: 如果明天下雨，我们就不去了 'If it rains tomorrow, we won't go'.
- 的话 can close the condition, with or without 如果: 你累的话，就休息一下 'If you are tired, take a rest'. Using both is fine: 如果你累的话，就休息一下.
- Do not put 就 in the condition part. It belongs to the result: 如果天气好，我们就去爬山 'If the weather is good, we will go climbing'.
- 你想去的话，就跟我们一起去 'If you want to go, then come with us' shows that 就 can also mean 'in that case'.
- (common mistake) Put the condition first: 如果明天下雨，我们不去. Do not start with the result.
- (common mistake) Do not put 就 in the condition: 如果你就有时间 is wrong. 就 goes in the result part.
- example: 如果明天下雨，我们就不去了。 / If it rains tomorrow, we won't go.
- example: 你累的话，就休息一下。 / If you are tired, take a rest.
- example: 没有钱的话，就不能买。 / If you have no money, you cannot buy it.
- example: 如果天气好，我们就去爬山。 / If the weather is good, we will go climbing.

### Lesson 4: 不但…而且…, 而且, 可是 and 然后
- 不但…而且… means 'not only … but also …': 这个菜不但便宜，而且好吃 'This dish is not only cheap but also delicious'. When both parts have the same subject, put 不但 after the subject: 她不但会唱歌，而且会跳舞.
- 而且 alone means 'moreover, and also': 今天很冷，而且下雨了 'It is cold today, and it has started raining'.
- 可是 means 'but': 这个很好看，可是有点贵 'This looks nice, but it is a bit expensive'. It is a little more spoken than 但是.
- 然后 means 'then, after that': 我们先看电影，然后去吃饭 'We will watch a movie first, and then go and eat'.
- (common mistake) Use 不但 with 而且, not with 但是 or 可是: 不但…而且… go together.
- (common mistake) 而且 comes at the start of the second part: do not put it before the first part.
- example: 这个饭馆不但很便宜，而且很好吃。 / This restaurant is not only cheap but also delicious.
- example: 今天很冷，而且下雨了。 / It is cold today, and it has started raining.
- example: 这个很好看，可是有点贵。 / This looks nice, but it is a bit expensive.
- example: 我们先看电影，然后去吃饭。 / We will watch a movie first, and then go and eat.

### Lesson 5: 只要…就… and 只有…才…
- 只要…就… means 'as long as … then …': 只要天气好，我们就去公园 'As long as the weather is good, we go to the park'. The condition is easy to meet, and the result follows.
- 只有…才… means 'only if … then …': 只有多练习，才能说好汉语 'Only by practising more can you speak Chinese well'. The condition is the one and only way.
- Compare: 只要你来，我就去 'As long as you come, I will go' (that is enough) and 只有你来，我才去 'I will go only if you come' (nobody else will do).
- 才 here means 'only then', and it goes before the verb of the result.
- (common mistake) Do not mix the pairs: 只要 goes with 就 and 只有 goes with 才. 只要…才… is wrong.
- (common mistake) The result part keeps its verb after 就 or 才: 只有你来，我才去, not 只有你来，才我去.
- example: 只要天气好，我们就去公园。 / As long as the weather is good, we go to the park.
- example: 只要多听，就能听懂。 / As long as you listen more, you will understand.
- example: 只有多说，才能说好。 / Only by speaking more can you speak well.
- example: 只有你来，我才去。 / I will go only if you come.

### Lesson 6: 除了…, 都 / 还…: except and besides
- 除了 A，都… means 'except A, everyone or everything …': 除了星期天，我每天都上班 'Except for Sunday, I go to work every day'; 除了他，大家都来了 'Everyone came except him'.
- 除了 A，还 / 也… means 'besides A, also …': 除了苹果，我还买了西瓜 'Apart from apples, I also bought watermelon'.
- 以外 is optional and goes after A: 除了他以外，大家都来了. It does not change the meaning.
- The ending shows which meaning you want: 都 for 'all the rest', 还 or 也 for 'in addition'.
- (common mistake) 除了 alone is not a full sentence. Finish it with 都 ('all the rest') or 还 / 也 ('in addition').
- (common mistake) Do not mix the two meanings: 除了…都… takes something away, 除了…还… adds something.
- example: 除了周末，我每天都学汉语。 / Except for weekends, I study Chinese every day.
- example: 除了米饭，我还想吃鱼。 / Besides rice, I also want to eat fish.
- example: 除了你以外，我谁都不认识。 / Except for you, I do not know anyone.
- example: 除了哥哥，大家都喜欢运动。 / Except for my older brother, everyone likes sports.

### Lesson 7: 为了, 为, 关于 and 根据
- 为了 + goal means 'in order to, for the sake of': 为了学好汉语，他每天都练习 'In order to learn Chinese well, he practises every day'. The goal comes first, then what you do.
- 为 + person + verb means 'for someone': 妈妈为孩子准备了早饭 'Mom prepared breakfast for the child'. 我为你高兴 means 'I am happy for you'.
- 关于 + topic means 'about': 我想看一个关于中国的电影 'I want to watch a movie about China'. 关于这件事，我不知道 'About this matter, I do not know'.
- 根据 means 'according to, based on': 根据地图，银行在前面 'According to the map, the bank is ahead'.
- (common mistake) 为了 gives a goal, 因为 gives a reason. Say 为了考试，他每天学习 'He studies every day to prepare for the exam', not 因为考试 when you mean a goal.
- (common mistake) 关于 needs a topic after it: 关于中国的书 is a book about China.
- example: 为了去中国学习，他在学汉语。 / In order to study in China, he is learning Chinese.
- example: 妈妈为孩子准备了早饭。 / Mom prepared breakfast for the child.
- example: 我想看一个关于中国的电影。 / I want to watch a movie about China.
- example: 根据他的话，火车晚点了。 / According to what he says, the train is late.

### Lesson 8: 或者 and 还是: two kinds of "or"
- 或者 means 'or' in a statement: 我想喝茶或者咖啡 'I want tea or coffee'; 周末我想看电影或者去公园 'At the weekend I want to watch a movie or go to the park'. It says either one is fine.
- 还是 means 'or' in a question where you ask the other person to choose: 你想喝茶还是咖啡？ 'Do you want tea or coffee?'
- In writing you may also see 或 on its own: 星期六或星期天.
- So: statement → 或者, choice question → 还是.
- (common mistake) Do not use 或者 in a question that asks someone to choose: say 你想喝茶还是咖啡？, not 你想喝茶或者咖啡？
- (common mistake) Do not use 还是 for 'or' in a plain statement of choices: say 我想喝茶或者咖啡, not 我想喝茶还是咖啡.
- example: 周末我想看电影或者去公园。 / At the weekend I want to watch a movie or go to the park.
- example: 明天或者后天，我都有时间。 / Either tomorrow or the day after tomorrow, I have time.
- example: 你想喝茶还是咖啡？ / Do you want tea or coffee?
- example: 我们坐火车或者坐飞机去。 / We will go by train or by plane.

### Lesson 9: 先…再…, 一边…一边… and 又…又…
- 先…再… means 'first … then …': 先洗手，再吃饭 'Wash your hands first, then eat'. 然后 can replace 再: 我们先看电影，然后去吃饭.
- 一边…一边… means 'while … (at the same time)': 妈妈一边做饭，一边听音乐 'Mom listens to music while she cooks'. 一边 goes before both verbs.
- 又…又… means 'both … and …' and joins two qualities: 这个菜又好吃又便宜 'This dish is both delicious and cheap'; 她又高又漂亮 'She is both tall and pretty'.
- (common mistake) Put 一边 before both verbs: 他一边吃饭一边看电视. Do not leave out the first 一边.
- (common mistake) 又…又… joins two things of the same kind (two adjectives or two verbs): 又高又漂亮.
- example: 先洗手，再吃饭。 / Wash your hands first, then eat.
- example: 妈妈一边做饭，一边听音乐。 / Mom cooks while listening to music.
- example: 这个菜又好吃又便宜。 / This dish is both delicious and cheap.
- example: 我先去银行，再去商店。 / I will go to the bank first, then to the shop.

### Lesson 10: 又 and 再: two kinds of "again"
- Both 又 and 再 mean 'again', but 又 is for something that has already happened again, and 再 is for something that will happen again.
- 又 looks back: 他昨天迟到，今天又迟到了 'He was late yesterday, and again today'. The sentence usually ends with 了.
- 再 looks ahead: 请再说一遍 'Please say it once more'; 明天我再来 'I will come again tomorrow'.
- A quick test: if you can say it with 'again, as it happened', use 又. If it is a request or a plan, use 再.
- (common mistake) Do not use 再 for something that already happened: say 今天又来了, not 今天再来了.
- (common mistake) Do not use 又 for a plan or a request: say 明天我再来, not 明天我又来.
- example: 他今天又迟到了。 / He was late again today.
- example: 请再说一遍。 / Please say it once more.
- example: 我昨天去了，今天又去了。 / I went yesterday, and I went again today.
- example: 明天我再来。 / I will come again tomorrow.

### Lesson 11: 越…越… and 越来越
- 越…越… means 'the more … the more …': 他越说越快 'He speaks faster and faster'; 汉语歌越听越好听 'This song sounds better the more you listen to it'.
- 越来越 + adjective (or a feeling verb such as 喜欢) means 'more and more': 天气越来越冷 'The weather gets colder and colder'; 我越来越喜欢汉语了 'I like Chinese more and more'. A final 了 is common.
- Both parts of 越 stand right before the words they describe. Do not add 很 after them.
- (common mistake) Put 越 before both words: 雨越下越大. 雨下越大 is wrong.
- (common mistake) Do not add 很 after 越来越: say 天气越来越冷, not 天气越来越很冷.
- example: 他越说越快。 / He speaks faster and faster.
- example: 汉语歌越听越好听。 / Chinese songs sound better the more you listen to them.
- example: 天气越来越冷了。 / The weather has become colder and colder.
- example: 我越来越喜欢汉语了。 / I have come to like Chinese more and more.

### Lesson 12: 才 and 就: late and early
- 才 after a time word means 'only then': it happened later than expected. 他八点才到学校 'He did not get to school until eight'. 我等了半个小时，他才来 'I waited half an hour before he came'.
- 就 after a time word means 'as early as': it happened sooner than expected. 他六点就起床了 'He got up as early as six'.
- So the same time word gives opposite feelings: 他六点才起床 (late) and 他六点就起床了 (early). A 就 sentence about the past normally ends with 了; a 才 sentence does not.
- 才 can also mean 'only' with a small amount or a time that is not late: 这件衣服才三十块 'This piece of clothing is only thirty yuan'; 才八点，别急 'It is only eight o'clock, don't worry'.
- (common mistake) 才 and 就 are not interchangeable. 他六点才起床 says he got up late; 他六点就起床了 says he got up early.
- (common mistake) Use 才 for late and 就 for early, and put both before the verb: 他八点才到, 他六点就到了. A 才 sentence about the past usually has no 了 at the end.
- example: 他八点才到学校。 / He did not get to school until eight.
- example: 他六点就起床了。 / He got up as early as six.
- example: 这件衣服才三十块。 / This piece of clothing is only thirty yuan.
- example: 我等了半个小时，他才来。 / I waited half an hour before he came.

### Lesson 13: 刚, 刚刚 and 刚才: just
- 刚 means 'just (a moment ago)': 我刚到家 'I have just got home'; 电影刚开始 'The film has just started'. It goes before the verb. 刚刚 is the same with more stress, 'only just': 他刚刚出去 'He has only just gone out'.
- 刚才 means 'just now'. It is a time word, so it can come before the subject: 刚才谁给你打电话？ 'Who called you just now?'
- 刚 cannot stand before the subject: say 我刚到, not 刚我到. Use 刚才 there.
- (common mistake) 刚 goes right before the verb. 刚我到家 is wrong; say 我刚到家 or 刚才我到家了.
- (common mistake) Do not use 刚 for the future. 我刚去 never means 'I will go'.
- example: 我刚吃完饭。 / I have just finished eating.
- example: 电影刚开始。 / The film has just started.
- example: 刚才谁给你打电话？ / Who called you just now?
- example: 他刚刚出去。 / He has only just gone out.

### Lesson 14: 一直, 总是, 常常 and 平时: always and often
- 一直 means 'all along, without stopping': 我一直在学汉语 'I have been studying Chinese all along'. With a direction it means 'straight': 一直往前走，银行就在左边 'Go straight ahead, and the bank is on the left'.
- 总是 (or 总) means 'always, every time': 他总是很早到学校 'He always gets to school very early'. 老 + verb is a casual way to complain: 他老迟到 'He is always late'.
- 常 and 常常 mean 'often': 我们常常在这里见面 'We often meet here'. 平时 and 一般 mean 'usually': 我平时七点起床 'I usually get up at seven'.
- All of these go before the verb, after the subject.
- (common mistake) Put these words before the verb: 我一直在这里等你, not 我在这里等你一直.
- (common mistake) 总是 means every time, 常常 means many times. Do not use 总是 when it is only 'often'.
- example: 我一直在学汉语。 / I have been studying Chinese all along.
- example: 一直往前走，银行就在左边。 / Go straight ahead, and the bank is on the left.
- example: 他总是很早到学校。 / He always gets to school very early.
- example: 我们常常在这里见面。 / We often meet here.

### Lesson 15: 更, 比较, 挺, 特别 and 极了: how much
- These words say how much, and each one already does the job of 很. 比较 means 'fairly, rather': 这个问题比较难. 挺 means 'quite', and is often followed by 的: 他的汉语挺好的 'His Chinese is quite good'. 特别 means 'especially, very': 这里的菜特别好吃 'The food here is especially delicious'.
- 更 means 'even more' and is used to compare: 这个比那个更大 'This one is even bigger than that one'.
- 极了 goes after the adjective and means 'extremely': 今天的天气好极了 'The weather today is extremely nice'; 我累极了 'I am tired out'.
- A rough scale from weak to strong: 比较, 挺, 很, 特别, 极了.
- (common mistake) Do not add 很 to these words: 很挺好 and 很特别好 are wrong. Each one already says how much.
- (common mistake) 极了 comes after the adjective: 好极了, not 极了好.
- example: 他的汉语挺好的。 / His Chinese is quite good.
- example: 这里的菜特别好吃。 / The food here is especially delicious.
- example: 今天的天气好极了。 / The weather today is extremely nice.
- example: 这个比那个更大。 / This one is even bigger than that one.

### Lesson 16: 一样, 不同 and more ways to compare
- A 跟 B 一样 means 'A is the same as B': 我的手机跟你的一样 'My phone is the same as yours'. Add an adjective for 'as … as': 他跟我一样高 'He is as tall as me'. The negative is 不一样: 这两个字不一样 'These two characters are not the same'. 不同 means 'different' and sounds a little more formal: 两个城市的天气不同.
- 比 + 更 or 还 means 'even more': 今天比昨天还冷 'Today is even colder than yesterday'. Do not add 很.
- 比 + adjective + 多了 means 'much more': 他比我高多了 'He is much taller than me'.
- 跟…比起来 means 'compared with …': 跟我的手机比起来，你的手机大多了 'Compared with my phone, yours is much bigger'.
- (common mistake) Do not add 很 in a 比 sentence: 他比我更高 is right, 他比我很更高 is wrong.
- (common mistake) In 跟…一样 the adjective comes after 一样: 他跟我一样高, not 他跟我高一样.
- example: 我的手机跟你的一样。 / My phone is the same as yours.
- example: 今天比昨天还冷。 / Today is even colder than yesterday.
- example: 他比我高多了。 / He is much taller than me.
- example: 这两个字不一样。 / These two characters are not the same.

### Lesson 17: 难 and 容易: hard to, easy to
- 难 + verb means 'hard to …': 这种鞋很难买 'This kind of shoes is hard to buy'; 汉语不难学 'Chinese is not hard to learn'. The thing you are talking about comes first, then 难, then the verb.
- 容易 + verb means 'easy to …': 这个问题很容易回答 'This question is easy to answer'. It can also mean 'likely to': 冬天容易感冒 'It is easy to catch a cold in winter'.
- Some of these have become words of their own: 难看 'ugly', 难听 'unpleasant to hear'. The opposite pattern with 好 is common too: 好看, 好吃, 好听.
- If you want to say how hard or easy, 很 goes before 难 or 容易.
- (common mistake) Put 很 before 难 or 容易, not after: 很难写, not 难很写.
- (common mistake) The thing you talk about usually comes first: 这个字很难写 sounds more natural than 很难写这个字.
- example: 汉语不难学。 / Chinese is not hard to learn.
- example: 这个问题很容易回答。 / This question is easy to answer.
- example: 这种鞋很难买。 / This kind of shoes is hard to buy.
- example: 冬天容易感冒。 / It is easy to catch a cold in winter.

### Lesson 18: 听得懂 and 听不懂: can or cannot
- Put 得 between a verb and its result to say you can reach that result, and 不 to say you cannot: 我听不懂 'I cannot understand (what I hear)'; 你看得清楚吗？ 'Can you see clearly?'
- Common ones: 听得懂 / 听不懂 (understand by listening), 看得见 / 看不见 (see), 吃得完 / 吃不完 (finish eating), 找得到 / 找不到 (find), 买得到 / 买不到 (be able to buy).
- In statements the 不 form is far more common: 这么多菜，我吃不完 'There is so much food that I cannot finish it'. The 得 form is mostly used in questions and answers.
- Use this pattern instead of 不能 + result: say 我听不懂, not 我不能听懂.
- (common mistake) Do not say 我不能听懂. For 'I cannot understand' say 我听不懂.
- (common mistake) The result comes after 得 or 不: 听不懂, not 听懂不.
- example: 老师说得太快，我听不懂。 / The teacher speaks too fast, and I cannot understand.
- example: 你看得清楚吗？ / Can you see clearly?
- example: 这么多菜，我吃不完。 / There is so much food that I cannot finish it.
- example: 我找不到我的手机。 / I cannot find my phone.

### Lesson 19: 起来, 出来, 下去 and 向
- After a verb, direction words such as 进 'in', 出 'out' and 回 'back' go in front of 来 or 去: 他走进来了 'He has walked in', 出去 'go out', 回来 'come back'. If there is a place, it goes between the two parts: 他回学校去了 'He has gone back to school'.
- 起来 can mean 'start to': 他笑起来了 'He started to laugh'. 下去 can mean 'go on': 请说下去 'Please go on speaking'.
- 向 + direction or person means 'toward': 孩子们向老师跑去了 'The children ran toward the teacher'; 请向前走 'Please walk forward'.
- (common mistake) Do not put a place after 来 or 去: say 他回学校去了, not 他回去学校了.
- (common mistake) Do not put a plain direction word like 起 on its own after a verb. It needs 来: 笑起来.
- example: 他笑起来了。 / He started to laugh.
- example: 请进来。 / Please come in.
- example: 请说下去。 / Please go on speaking.
- example: 孩子们向老师跑去了。 / The children ran toward the teacher.

### Lesson 20: How long: 学了两年 and 三年没见了
- To say how long something lasted, put the time length after the verb and 了: 我学了两年汉语 'I studied Chinese for two years'. The time length comes before the object.
- Add a second 了 at the end if it is still going on: 他在这里工作了三年了 'He has worked here for three years (and still does)'.
- Time length + 没 + verb + 了 means 'has not … for …': 我们三年没见面了 'We have not met for three years'.
- The time length never goes before the verb: 我两年学汉语 is wrong.
- (common mistake) Time length goes after the verb, not before: 我学了两年汉语, not 我两年学汉语.
- (common mistake) Without the final 了 the action is finished; with it, the action is still going on.
- example: 我学了两年汉语。 / I studied Chinese for two years.
- example: 他在这里工作了三年了。 / He has worked here for three years.
- example: 我们三年没见面了。 / We have not met for three years.
- example: 他等了你一个小时。 / He waited for you for an hour.

### Lesson 21: 以前, 以后, 后来 and more time words
- 以前 means 'before' and 以后 means 'after'. They come after a time or a verb: 八点以前 'before eight'; 睡觉以前 'before going to bed'; 吃饭以后 'after eating'. On their own they also mean 'in the past' and 'later, in the future'.
- 后来 means 'afterwards' and is only used about the past: 他以前不喝咖啡，后来喝了 'He did not drink coffee before; later he did'. For the future use 以后: 以后我想去中国.
- 然后 means 'then, after that', the next step in a series (past or future). 最后 means 'finally, last': 最后一个问题.
- More time words: 前天 'the day before yesterday', 后天 'the day after tomorrow', 前年 'the year before last', 后年 'the year after next', 最近 'recently'.
- (common mistake) 后来 is only for the past. For the future say 以后: 以后我想去中国, not 后来我想去中国.
- (common mistake) 以前 and 以后 come after the time or verb: 八点以前, not 以前八点.
- example: 八点以前，我必须到。 / I must arrive before eight.
- example: 睡觉以前，他喜欢听音乐。 / Before going to bed, he likes listening to music.
- example: 他以前不喝咖啡，后来喝了。 / He did not drink coffee before; later he did.
- example: 我前天见到了他。 / I saw him the day before yesterday.

### Lesson 22: 应该, 必须, 需要, 不用 and 最好
- 应该 (or 该) means 'should': 你应该多休息 'You should rest more'. The negative is 不应该.
- 必须 means 'must, have to': 学生必须在八点以前到学校 'Students must be at school before eight'. Do not say 不必须. The opposite of 必须 is 不用, 'no need to': 你不用急，慢慢来 'There is no need to rush; take your time'.
- 需要 is a verb that means 'need': 我需要你的帮助 'I need your help'. It can also be followed by a verb: 你需要多休息.
- 最好 means 'had better': 外面很冷，你最好多穿一点 'It is cold outside, so you had better wear a bit more'.
- (common mistake) Do not say 不必须. The opposite of 必须 is 不用: 你不用来, not 你不必须来.
- (common mistake) 应该 and 该 both mean should. 该 is shorter and more common in speech.
- example: 你应该多休息。 / You should rest more.
- example: 学生必须在八点以前到学校。 / Students must be at school before eight.
- example: 你不用急，慢慢来。 / There is no need to rush; take your time.
- example: 外面很冷，你最好多穿一点。 / It is cold outside, so you had better wear a bit more.

### Lesson 23: 只, 只是 and 只能: only
- 只 before a verb means 'only': 我只喝水 'I only drink water'; 他只会说汉语 'He can only speak Chinese'. It goes right before the verb.
- 只是 means 'just, merely': 我只是想帮你 'I just want to help you'; 这只是一个小问题 'This is only a small problem'.
- 只能 means 'can only, have no choice but to': 没有别的办法，我们只能等 'There is no other way; we can only wait'.
- Do not confuse the 'only' 只 (zhǐ) with the measure word 只 (zhī) in 一只猫.
- (common mistake) 只 goes right before the verb: 我只喝水, not 我喝只水.
- (common mistake) 只有 is a different word: it needs 才 in the next part (see 只要 and 只有).
- example: 我只喝水。 / I only drink water.
- example: 他只会说汉语。 / He can only speak Chinese.
- example: 我只是想帮你。 / I just want to help you.
- example: 没有别的办法，我们只能等。 / There is no other way; we can only wait.

### Lesson 24: 好像, 看起来, 大概, 其实 and 当然
- 好像 means 'it seems, as if': 他好像不在家 'He does not seem to be at home'. 看起来 means 'it looks like': 这个菜看起来很好吃 'This dish looks delicious'. 看来 means 'it seems (from what I can see)': 看来明天要下雨 'It seems it will rain tomorrow'.
- 大概 means 'probably, roughly': 现在大概九点了 'It is probably about nine now'.
- 其实 means 'actually, in fact' and points out how things really are, often against what people think: 其实我不是老师 'Actually, I am not a teacher'.
- 当然 means 'of course': 你会游泳吗？——我当然会 'Can you swim? Of course I can'.
- (common mistake) 好像 here means 'seems', not 'like'. For 'like' (be fond of) use 喜欢.
- (common mistake) 其实 points to the real situation, so it often goes against what was just said. Do not use it just to agree.
- example: 他好像不在家。 / He does not seem to be at home.
- example: 这个菜看起来很好吃。 / This dish looks delicious.
- example: 现在大概九点了。 / It is probably about nine now.
- example: 其实我不是老师。 / Actually, I am not a teacher.

### Lesson 25: 终于, 几乎, 突然, 马上 and 一定
- 终于 means 'at last, finally' and is for something you waited for: 火车终于来了 'The train has finally come'. 几乎 means 'almost, nearly': 我几乎忘了他的名字 'I almost forgot his name'; 几乎没 means 'hardly any': 他几乎没吃东西.
- 突然 means 'suddenly': 突然，他笑了 'Suddenly, he laughed'; 天突然下雨了 'It suddenly started to rain'. It can go before the subject or before the verb.
- 马上 means 'right away': 我马上就到 'I will be there right away'. 一定 means 'certainly, must': 我一定会给你打电话 'I will definitely call you'.
- All of these go right before the verb; 突然 can also start the sentence.
- (common mistake) Use 终于 only for a result you were waiting for. For a surprise, say 突然.
- (common mistake) Put these words before the verb: 我马上就到, not 我到马上.
- example: 火车终于来了。 / The train has finally come.
- example: 我几乎忘了他的名字。 / I almost forgot his name.
- example: 突然，他笑了。 / Suddenly, he laughed.
- example: 我一定会给你打电话。 / I will definitely call you.

### Lesson 26: Question words for "any": 谁都, 想吃什么就吃什么, 一个也不
- A question word + 都 or 也 means 'every' or 'any': 谁都知道这件事 'Everyone knows about this'; 我哪里都不想去 'I don't want to go anywhere'. With 不 or 没 it means 'nothing, nobody, nowhere': 我什么都没买 'I did not buy anything'.
- 想 + question word + 就 + the same question word means 'as you like': 你想吃什么就吃什么 'Eat whatever you want'; 你想去哪里就去哪里 'Go wherever you want'.
- 一 + measure word + noun + 也 or 都 + 不 or 没 + verb means 'not even one': 教室里一个人也没有 'There is not a single person in the classroom'; 我一个字也不认识 'I do not know a single character'.
- (common mistake) Do not leave out 都 or 也: 谁知道这件事？ is a question 'Who knows?', but 谁都知道这件事 is a statement 'Everyone knows'.
- (common mistake) In 想…就… use the same question word twice: 想吃什么就吃什么.
- example: 谁都知道这件事。 / Everyone knows about this.
- example: 我什么都没买。 / I did not buy anything.
- example: 你想吃什么就吃什么。 / Eat whatever you want.
- example: 教室里一个人也没有。 / There is not a single person in the classroom.

### Lesson 27: 半, 两三个 and 以上 / 以下
- 半 means 'half'. After a measure word: 一个半小时 'one and a half hours'. Before a measure word: 半个小时 'half an hour'; 半年 'half a year'.
- Two numbers next to each other (smaller first) mean 'about': 两三个学生 'two or three students'; 我在这里住了七八天 'I stayed here for seven or eight days'.
- 以上 means 'above, and over' and counts the number itself: 十八岁以上的人 'people aged eighteen and over'. 以下 means 'below, under': 五十块以下的鞋 'shoes under fifty yuan'.
- (common mistake) 半 goes before the measure word in 半个小时 but after it in 一个半小时. Do not say 一半个小时.
- (common mistake) Put the smaller number first: 两三个, not 三两个.
- example: 我等了一个半小时。 / I waited for an hour and a half.
- example: 半个小时以后，他就到。 / He will be here in half an hour.
- example: 这里有两三个学生。 / There are two or three students here.
- example: 五十块以下的鞋很少。 / Shoes under fifty yuan are rare.

### Lesson 28: Words you know, with a new meaning
- Some words you already know come back in HSK 3 with another meaning or another sound. The character is the same; the word around it tells you which one it is.
- 得 děi means 'must, have to' before a verb: 我得走了 'I have to go now'; 你得多休息 'You must rest more'. It sounds different from the 得 after a verb (他跑得很快, de), and from 得 dé 'to get' (得到, 得分).
- 地 dì means 'ground, place': 地方 'place', 地铁 'subway', 地图 'map', 地上 'on the ground'. It is de only after an adverb (高兴地说). 过去 is also 'the past': 过去的事 'things of the past'.
- 还 huán means 'to give back': 还书 'return a book', 还钱 'pay back', 还给 'give back to'. 还 hái 'still, also' is the one you already know. 会 huì also means 'a meeting': 开会 'have a meeting'. 长 zhǎng means 'to grow': 长大 'grow up', 长高 'grow taller'; 长 cháng is 'long'.
- (common mistake) Do not mix up the sounds: 我得走了 is děi (must), 他跑得很快 is de (how). The characters are the same, the sound is decided by the words around.
- (common mistake) 还 hái 'still' goes before another verb: 我还没吃 'I have not eaten yet'. 还 huán 'give back' is the verb itself: 还书 'return a book'.
- example: 我得走了。 / I have to go now.
- example: 我把书还给他了。 / I have given the book back to him.
- example: 明天我们开会。 / We have a meeting tomorrow.
- example: 孩子长大了。 / The child has grown up.
