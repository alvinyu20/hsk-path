Hello! These are word groups from my HSK study site (HSK 3.0, levels 1 to 5). In each group the words used to have exactly the same English meaning, so learners could not tell them apart. Each word now has a distinguishing label (in brackets) and a usage note saying when to use which, with short examples.
Please act as a native speaker and Chinese teacher. **Report only the problems**, no praise. For each problem give: group number / the word / what is wrong (simple Chinese plus one line of English) / a more accurate version.
Please watch for: 1) a wrong distinction (a word called written, northern or spoken when it is not); 2) unnatural or wrong examples; 3) English that does not match the Chinese; 4) a more important difference that was missed; 5) words that are in fact interchangeable where the note says they are not, or the reverse. Chinese examples in a note may only use words at or below that word's level, so a lower-level word sometimes does not name its higher-level partner: that is on purpose.
If there are no problems, answer "No problems found".

## 1. to swim

- **游** (yóu, HSK 2): to swim (how, where or how far)
  - Note: 游 is the plain verb, used when something comes after it: 游得很快 (swims fast), 游到这里 (swims over here). It is also what fish do. For swimming as an activity or sport, say 游泳: 我去游泳.
  - Example: 他游得很快。 / He swims fast.
- **游泳** (yóuyǒng, HSK 2): to swim; swimming (the activity, the sport)
  - Note: 游泳 is swimming as an activity: 去游泳 (go swimming), 会游泳 (can swim), 喜欢游泳. To say how or where you swim, use 游: 游得很快, not 游泳得很快.
  - Example: 我喜欢游泳。 / I like swimming.

## 2. breakfast

- **早饭** (zǎofàn, HSK 1): breakfast (everyday)
  - Example: 我七点吃早饭。 / I eat breakfast at seven.
- **早餐** (zǎocān, HSK 4): breakfast (more formal: menus, hotels)
  - Note: 早餐 is more formal: on menus, in hotels and in writing: 早餐时间 (breakfast time), 酒店早餐. At home people usually say 早饭: 我七点吃早饭.
  - Example: 早餐我吃面包。 / I eat bread for breakfast.

## 3. busy

- **忙** (máng, HSK 1): busy (everyday)
  - Example: 我今天很忙。 / I am very busy today.
- **忙碌** (mánglù, HSK 5): busy (written; busy for a long time)
  - Note: 忙碌 means busy and occupied, often for a long time: 忙碌的一天 (a busy day). It sounds a little more literary than 忙.
  - Example: 他每天都很忙碌。 / He is very busy every day.

## 4. child

- **孩子** (háizi, HSK 1): child (also: someone's own kids)
  - Note: 孩子 is the general word for a child, and the word for your own sons and daughters: 你有孩子吗? (Do you have children?)
  - Example: 这个孩子五岁。 / This child is five years old.
- **小孩儿** (xiǎoháir, HSK 2): child (little kid, casual)
  - Note: 小孩儿 is a casual word for a small child: 一个小孩儿. 孩子 is the general word, and the one for your own children: 我有两个孩子.
  - Example: 这个小孩儿很高兴。 / This child is very happy.

## 5. Chinese (the language)

- **汉语** (Hànyǔ, HSK 1): Chinese (the language; in textbooks)
  - Note: 汉语 and 中文 both mean 'Chinese (the language)', and you can use either one for speaking: 我会说汉语 and 我会说中文 are both natural. 汉语 sounds a little more formal, like a school subject: 汉语课. The HSK itself is short for Hànyǔ Shuǐpíng Kǎoshì.
  - Example: 你的汉语很好。 / Your Chinese is very good.
- **中文** (Zhōngwén, HSK 1): Chinese (the language; everyday)
  - Note: 中文 is the everyday word for 'Chinese', for speaking and for writing: 我会说中文, 我会写中文. 汉语 means the same. The 文 in 中文 comes from 'writing', but that does not mean 中文 is only written: you do not have to switch words between speaking and writing.
  - Example: 你会写中文吗？ / Can you write Chinese?

## 6. computer

- **电脑** (diànnǎo, HSK 1): computer (everyday)
  - Example: 这是我的电脑。 / This is my computer.
- **计算机** (jìsuànjī, HSK 5): computer (formal; the subject)
  - Note: 计算机 is the formal word for a computer. In speech people also say 电脑.
  - Example: 他在计算机公司工作。 / He works at a computer company.

## 7. doctor

- **医生** (yīshēng, HSK 1): doctor (standard)
  - Example: 医生在医院工作。 / The doctor works at the hospital.
- **大夫** (dàifu, HSK 4): doctor (spoken, northern)
  - Note: 大夫 is read dàifu (not dàfū). It is a spoken word for a doctor, common in northern China.
  - Example: 昨天大夫说我需要多休息。 / Yesterday the doctor said I need to rest more.

## 8. here

- **这儿** (zhèr, HSK 1): here (spoken, northern)
  - Note: 这儿 is the spoken northern form of 这里. They mean the same: 我在这儿 = 我在这里. 这里 is used everywhere, in speech and in writing.
  - Example: 我在这儿。 / I am here.
- **这里** (zhèlǐ, HSK 1): here (standard)
  - Note: Standard pinyin has 里 in the third tone (zhèlǐ), but in everyday speech the 里 is often said lightly (zhèli). Both are right.
  - Example: 这里有很多人。 / There are a lot of people here.

## 9. inside

- **里** (lǐ, HSK 1): inside; in (after a noun: 房间里)
  - Note: 里 always comes after a noun: 房间里 (in the room), 杯子里 (in the cup).
  - Example: 杯子里有水。 / There is water in the cup.
- **里面** (lǐmiàn, HSK 2): inside (can stand alone)
  - Note: 里面 can stand on its own or come after a noun: 里面有很多人, 房间里面. 里 alone must follow a noun: 房间里.
  - Example: 里面有很多人。 / There are many people inside.
- **里头** (lǐtou, HSK 5): inside (casual, spoken)
  - Note: 里头 means "inside", the same as 里面 but more casual: 房间里头, 里头有人.
  - Example: 盒子里头有什么？ / What is inside the box?

## 10. lunch

- **午饭** (wǔfàn, HSK 1): lunch (everyday)
  - Example: 我们在学校吃午饭。 / We have lunch at school.
- **午餐** (wǔcān, HSK 4): lunch (more formal: menus, canteens)
  - Note: 午餐 is more formal: on menus, in canteens and in writing: 午餐时间 (lunchtime). At home and with friends people usually say 午饭: 一起吃午饭吧.
  - Example: 午餐我们吃面条。 / We eat noodles for lunch.

## 11. outside

- **外边** (wàibian, HSK 1): outside (spoken)
  - Example: 外边在下雨。 / It is raining outside.
- **外** (wài, HSK 1): outside (after a noun: 房间外)
  - Note: 外 alone is short and usually comes after a noun: 房间外 (outside the room). On its own in a sentence, say 外边: 外边很冷.
  - Example: 他不在房间里，在房间外边。 / He is not in the room, he is outside it.
- **外面** (wàimiàn, HSK 2): outside (speech and writing)
  - Note: 外面 and 外边 mean the same and are both common: 外面很冷 = 外边很冷. 外面 is a little more usual in writing.
  - Example: 外面很冷。 / It is cold outside.

## 12. restaurant

- **饭店** (fàndiàn, HSK 1): restaurant (also: hotel, in names)
  - Note: In names, 饭店 is often a hotel rather than a restaurant (for example, the Beijing Hotel).
  - Example: 我们在饭店吃饭。 / We eat at a restaurant.
- **饭馆** (fànguǎn, HSK 2): restaurant (small, everyday)
  - Note: 饭馆 is a small, everyday restaurant: 去饭馆吃饭. 饭店 can be a bigger restaurant, and in names it is often a hotel.
  - Example: 这个饭馆的饺子很好吃。 / The dumplings at this restaurant are delicious.

## 13. room

- **房间** (fángjiān, HSK 1): room (in a home or hotel)
  - Example: 我的房间很小。 / My room is very small.
- **室** (shì, HSK 3): room (in words: 教室, 办公室)
  - Note: 室 means a room and is used in words like 教室 and 办公室.
  - Example: 教室里没有人。 / There is nobody in the classroom.

## 14. song

- **歌** (gē, HSK 1): song (everyday)
  - Example: 你喜欢听歌吗？ / Do you like listening to songs?
- **歌曲** (gēqǔ, HSK 5): song (formal, written)
  - Note: 歌曲 is a formal word for a song. In speech people say 歌 or 一首歌.
  - Example: 他很会唱中国歌曲。 / He is very good at singing Chinese songs.

## 15. steamed bun

- **包子** (bāozi, HSK 1): steamed bun (with a filling)
  - Note: 包子 has a filling inside, often meat or vegetables.
  - Example: 我想吃两个包子。 / I want to eat two buns.
- **馒头** (mántou, HSK 4): steamed bun (plain, no filling)
  - Note: 馒头 is a plain steamed bun with no filling. A filled one is 包子.
  - Example: 早饭我吃了两个馒头。 / I ate two steamed buns for breakfast.

## 16. teacher

- **老师** (lǎoshī, HSK 1): teacher (also how you address one)
  - Note: 老师 is also what you call a teacher, alone or after their family name: 老师好! (Hello, teacher!)
  - Example: 她是我的老师。 / She is my teacher.
- **教师** (jiàoshī, HSK 4): teacher (job title, formal)
  - Note: 教师 is formal or written, the name of the job: 小学教师 (a primary-school teacher). In speech, and when you talk to a teacher, say 老师: 老师好!
  - Example: 她是一位小学教师。 / She is a primary school teacher.

## 17. there

- **那儿** (nàr, HSK 1): there (spoken, northern)
  - Note: 那儿 is the spoken northern form of 那里. They mean the same. 那里 is used everywhere, in speech and in writing.
  - Example: 你在那儿做什么？ / What are you doing there?
- **那里** (nàlǐ, HSK 1): there (standard)
  - Note: Standard pinyin has 里 in the third tone (nàlǐ), but in everyday speech the 里 is often said lightly (nàli). Both are right.
  - Example: 那里有一家商店。 / There is a shop over there.

## 18. to open; to turn on

- **开** (kāi, HSK 1): to open; to turn on (also: to drive)
  - Note: 开 has many uses: 开电视 (to turn on the TV), 开车 (to drive).
  - Example: 我想开电视看电影。 / I want to turn on the TV and watch a movie.
- **打开** (dǎkāi, HSK 2): to open; to turn on (+ the thing)
  - Note: 打开 needs the thing after it: 打开书 (to open a book), 打开电视. 开 is shorter and has more uses: 开门, 开车 (to drive).
  - Example: 请打开书。 / Please open your book.

## 19. to sell

- **卖** (mài, HSK 1): to sell (everyday)
  - Example: 这家店卖水果。 / This shop sells fruit.
- **销售** (xiāoshòu, HSK 5): to sell (business: sales)
  - Note: 销售 is to sell, often by a company: 销售电脑 (to sell computers), 做销售 (to work in sales).
  - Example: 这家公司销售电脑。 / This company sells computers.

## 20. today

- **今天** (jīntiān, HSK 1): today (everyday word)
  - Example: 今天很冷。 / It is very cold today.
- **今日** (jīnrì, HSK 5): today (written: news, signs)
  - Note: 今日 is a written word for 今天, used in news and on signs: 今日新闻 (today's news). In speech people say 今天.
  - Example: 今日的天气很好。 / Today's weather is very good.

## 21. train

- **火车** (huǒchē, HSK 1): train (everyday)
  - Example: 他坐火车回家。 / He takes the train home.
- **列车** (lièchē, HSK 5): train (announcements, formal)
  - Note: 列车 is a train, used in announcements and formal speech: 高速列车 (a high-speed train), 列车到站 (the train arrives at the station).
  - Example: 列车马上就要开了。 / The train is about to leave.

## 22. week

- **星期** (xīngqī, HSK 1): week (with day names: 星期一)
  - Note: 星期 + a number names the days: 星期一 (Monday), 星期六 (Saturday). Sunday is 星期天 or 星期日. 星期几 asks which day it is.
  - Example: 今天星期几？ / What day of the week is it today?
- **周** (zhōu, HSK 2): week (short form: 这周, 周末)
  - Note: 周 and 星期 both mean "week". 周 is shorter: 这周 (this week), 下周 (next week), 每周 (every week), and on calendars 周一 is Monday. In everyday speech the days are usually 星期一, 星期二.
  - Example: 这周我很忙。 / I am very busy this week.

## 23. where

- **哪儿** (nǎr, HSK 1): where (spoken, northern)
  - Note: 哪儿 is the spoken northern form of 哪里. They mean the same: 你去哪儿? = 你去哪里? 哪里 is used everywhere, in speech and in writing.
  - Example: 你家在哪儿？ / Where is your home?
- **哪里** (nǎlǐ, HSK 1): where (standard)
  - Note: Standard pinyin has 里 in the third tone (nǎlǐ), but in everyday speech the 里 is often said lightly (nǎli). Both are right.
  - Example: 你在哪里学习汉语？ / Where do you study Chinese?

## 24. already

- **已经** (yǐjīng, HSK 2): already (everyday)
  - Example: 他已经走了。 / He has already left.
- **已** (yǐ, HSK 4): already (formal, written)
  - Note: 已 is formal and written. In speech, 已经 is used.
  - Example: 比赛已结束。 / The match has already ended.

## 25. class (a group of students)

- **班** (bān, HSK 2): class (a group of students: 我们班)
  - Note: 班 is the everyday word: 我们班 (our class), 一个班. It is also in 上班 (to go to work).
  - Example: 我们班有二十个学生。 / Our class has twenty students.
- **班级** (bānjí, HSK 3): class (a group of students; formal)
  - Note: 班级 is the formal word for a class as a group. In speech people often say 班.
  - Example: 我们班级有三十个学生。 / Our class has thirty students.

## 26. husband

- **丈夫** (zhàngfu, HSK 2): husband (standard)
  - Example: 她的丈夫是医生。 / Her husband is a doctor.
- **老公** (lǎogōng, HSK 5): husband (casual)
  - Note: 老公 is a casual word for husband, used by wives. A more formal word is 丈夫.
  - Example: 我老公今天不在家。 / My husband is not at home today.

## 27. often

- **经常** (jīngcháng, HSK 2): often (frequently)
  - Note: 经常 means often, again and again: 我经常看电影. The negative is 不经常 or 不常.
  - Example: 我经常看电影。 / I often watch movies.
- **常** (cháng, HSK 3): often (short, a little written)
  - Note: 常 is the short form, a little more written: 我们常见面. 常常 and 经常 are more common in speech. The negative is 不常: 我不常去.
  - Example: 我们常在这里见面。 / We often meet here.
- **常常** (chángcháng, HSK 3): often (spoken)
  - Note: 常常 and 经常 both mean often and are common in speech. The negative is 不常, not 不常常: 我不常去.
  - Example: 他常常迟到。 / He is often late.

## 28. online

- **网上** (wǎngshàng, HSK 2): online (on the internet)
  - Note: 网上 is "on the internet", the everyday word: 在网上买东西 (to shop online).
  - Example: 我在网上买东西。 / I buy things online.
- **线上** (xiànshàng, HSK 4): online (as opposed to in person)
  - Note: 线上 is "online" as opposed to in person, about classes, events and shops: 线上学习. For "on the internet" in everyday speech, say 网上: 在网上买东西.
  - Example: 这门课可以线上学习。 / This course can be studied online.
- **在线** (zàixiàn, HSK 5): online (connected right now)
  - Note: 在线 is being connected right now, like a status: 他在线吗? (Is he online?) It is also in 在线学习 (to learn online).
  - Example: 他现在在线吗？ / Is he online now?

## 29. red

- **红色** (hóngsè, HSK 2): red (the colour, as a noun)
  - Note: 红色 is the colour as a noun: 红色的衣服 (red clothes), 我喜欢红色.
  - Example: 她穿着红色的衣服。 / She is wearing red clothes.
- **红** (hóng, HSK 5): red (adjective; also: popular)
  - Note: 红 means red: 红花, 脸红了 (to blush). It is also used for popular: 他很红 (he is very popular).
  - Example: 苹果熟了，变红了。 / The apple is ripe and has turned red.

## 30. slow

- **慢** (màn, HSK 2): slow (everyday)
  - Example: 请说慢一点儿。 / Please speak a bit slower.
- **缓慢** (huǎnmàn, HSK 5): slow (written; slow movement)
  - Note: 缓慢 is a written word for slow, usually about movement or speed. The everyday word is 慢.
  - Example: 火车缓慢地向前行驶。 / The train moves forward slowly.

## 31. to enter

- **进** (jìn, HSK 2): to enter (everyday: 进来, 请进)
  - Note: 进 is the everyday verb: 请进 (come in), 进来 (to come in), 进去 (to go in).
  - Example: 请进。 / Please come in.
- **进入** (jìnrù, HSK 4): to enter (formal; also a new stage)
  - Note: 进入 is more formal and needs a place or a stage after it: 进入房间, 进入大学 (to start university). In speech, 进 is more common: 进来, 请进.
  - Example: 请从这个门进入。 / Please enter through this door.
- **入** (rù, HSK 4): to enter (in words: 入口, 加入)
  - Note: 入 is mostly used inside words: 入口 (entrance), 加入 (to join). For "to go in" in speech, say 进: 请进.
  - Example: 请问，入口在哪里？ / Excuse me, where is the entrance?

## 32. to forget

- **忘** (wàng, HSK 2): to forget (short, spoken)
  - Example: 我忘了他的名字。 / I forgot his name.
- **忘记** (wàngjì, HSK 3): to forget (a little fuller)
  - Note: 忘记 and 忘 mean the same. 忘 is shorter and very common in speech: 我忘了. 忘记 is a little fuller and fine in speech or writing: 别忘记带手机.
  - Example: 我忘记带手机了。 / I forgot to bring my phone.

## 33. to go out

- **出** (chū, HSK 2): to go out; to come out (+ a place)
  - Note: 出 usually needs something after it: 出门 (to go out the door), 出来 (to come out).
  - Example: 他出门了。 / He went out.
- **出去** (chūqù, HSK 2): to go out (can stand alone)
  - Note: 出去 can stand on its own: 我出去一下 (I'm stepping out). 出 needs something after it: 出门.
  - Example: 我想出去玩。 / I want to go out and play.
- **外出** (wàichū, HSK 4): to go out (away for a while; formal)
  - Note: 外出 is formal: going out of the home or office for a while, often for work or a trip: 外出工作. In speech, people say 出去.
  - Example: 他外出工作了。 / He has gone out to work.

## 34. to help

- **帮** (bāng, HSK 2): to help (do something for someone)
  - Note: 帮 is common for a quick favour: 帮我一下 (give me a hand).
  - Example: 请你帮我一下。 / Please help me.
- **帮助** (bāngzhù, HSK 3): to help; help (also a noun)
  - Note: 帮助 is also a noun: 谢谢你的帮助 (thanks for your help). 帮 is only a verb and is common for a quick favour: 帮我一下.
  - Example: 老师常常帮助我学习汉语。 / The teacher often helps me study Chinese.

## 35. together

- **一起** (yìqǐ, HSK 2): together (standard)
  - Example: 我们一起去吧。 / Let us go together.
- **一块儿** (yíkuàir, HSK 3): together (spoken, northern)
  - Note: 一块儿 is a spoken northern word for 一起: 我们一块儿去吧. 一起 is used everywhere, in speech and in writing.
  - Example: 我们一块儿去吧。 / Let us go together.

## 36. wife

- **妻子** (qīzi, HSK 2): wife (standard)
  - Example: 他的妻子是老师。 / His wife is a teacher.
- **老婆** (lǎopo, HSK 5): wife (casual)
  - Note: 老婆 is a casual word for wife, used by husbands. A more formal word is 妻子.
  - Example: 他的老婆很漂亮。 / His wife is very beautiful.

## 37. actually; in fact

- **其实** (qíshí, HSK 3): actually; in fact (the truth is…)
  - Note: 其实 tells the real situation, often against what was expected: 其实这个字不难.
  - Example: 其实这个字不难写。 / Actually, this character is not hard to write.
- **实际上** (shíjìshàng, HSK 4): actually; in fact (in reality; more formal)
  - Note: 实际上 means "in reality", often after how something looks: 看起来年轻，实际上五十岁了. 其实 is more common in speech.
  - Example: 他看起来年轻，实际上已经五十岁了。 / He looks young, but actually he is already 50.

## 38. almost; nearly

- **几乎** (jīhū, HSK 3): almost; nearly (very close to all)
  - Note: 几乎 means very close to all or none: 几乎每天 (almost every day). For something that nearly happened but did not, use 差点儿.
  - Example: 他几乎每天都来这里。 / He comes here almost every day.
- **差点儿** (chàdiǎnr, HSK 4): almost; nearly (but it didn't happen)
  - Note: 差点儿 is for something that nearly happened but did not: 我差点儿忘了 (I almost forgot). For "almost all", use 几乎: 几乎每天.
  - Example: 我差点儿忘了今天是你的生日。 / I almost forgot that today is your birthday.

## 39. altogether; in total

- **一共** (yígòng, HSK 3): altogether; in total (everyday)
  - Example: 一共多少钱？ / How much is it altogether?
- **共** (gòng, HSK 4): altogether; in total (formal, written)
  - Note: 共 is the short, written form of 一共: 我们班共有三十个学生. In speech, say 一共: 一共多少钱?
  - Example: 我们班共有三十个学生。 / Our class has thirty students altogether.

## 40. and also; moreover

- **而且** (érqiě, HSK 3): and also; moreover (everyday)
  - Example: 这个菜很好吃，而且很便宜。 / This dish is delicious, and it is cheap too.
- **并且** (bìngqiě, HSK 4): and also; moreover (formal; links actions)
  - Note: 并且 is a little more formal than 而且 and often links two actions of the same person: 他会说汉语，并且说得很好. In speech, 而且 is more common.
  - Example: 他会说汉语，并且说得很好。 / He can speak Chinese, and speaks it very well.
